Why heritage walks keep visitors longer in Kathmandu

12 March 2026 / Heritage / 4 min read

A short guided walk can add a night to a stay. The Kathmandu city core shows how.

Visitors often see the old city in a morning and leave by noon. A walk with a clear route, a guide who knows the courtyards and a few stops for tea changes that. People stay longer, spend in small shops and talk about what they saw.

What the walks need

The plan for a heritage walk is simple. It needs a route that is safe on foot, a guide trained in the history of each stop, and a fair share of income for the families who live along it.

  • A fixed route with signs in two languages
  • Trained local guides with a shared code of conduct
  • Small groups, so the street stays a street

A heritage walk works when the neighbours want it to.

Why it matters for conservation

Walks give owners a reason to keep old houses in repair. They also give the city a count of visitors, which helps when budgets are set. See the heritage walks project for the background.
